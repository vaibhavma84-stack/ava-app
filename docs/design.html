Design proposal · Android

Earmark: turn recordings into text, entirely on the phone

Record a conversation, open an audio file, or pick a video, and get a searchable, timestamped transcript. Speech recognition runs on the device, so it works with no signal and nothing leaves the phone.

Platform Android 9+ (API 28)Engine Whisper, on-deviceLanguage EnglishShips as a standalone appStack Kotlin · Jetpack ComposeStatus Draft for review

What it must do

Work offline

After a one-time model download, recording, transcription, search and export need no internet connection.

Take any audio

Record in the app, pick an audio file (MP3, M4A, WAV, OGG, OPUS, FLAC), or share a voice note in from WhatsApp or Recorder.

Take video too

Pick or share in a video (MP4, MKV, WebM, 3GP, MOV) from the gallery or WhatsApp. The app pulls out the sound track and transcribes that; export as subtitles to put words back on the video.

Keep text tied to sound

Every sentence carries its timestamp. Tap a line to hear it; edit words that were misheard.

Get it out

Copy, share, or export as plain text, SRT or VTT subtitles, with or without timestamps.

Screens

Four screens cover the whole job. Material 3 components, one accent colour, and red reserved for "recording in progress".

How transcription works

A file of any length goes through the same five steps. The work runs in a background job with a progress notification, so the user can lock the phone or switch apps and it keeps going.

STEP 1

Decode

MediaExtractor finds the audio track (in an audio file or a video); MediaCodec turns AAC, MP3, Opus, AC-3 and the rest into raw PCM. Video frames are never decoded.

STEP 2

Resample

Convert to 16 kHz mono float, the format Whisper expects.

STEP 3

Find speech

Silero VAD skips silence and splits audio into ≤30 s chunks at pauses.

STEP 4

Recognise

whisper.cpp (native, via JNI) returns text with per-segment and per-word timings.

STEP 5

Save

Segments land in the database as they finish, so partial results appear early.

Choosing the model

The app is English only, so it uses Whisper's English-only models, which are slightly more accurate for English than the multilingual ones of the same size. Bigger is more accurate and slower. The app ships without a model and downloads one on first run; the user can switch later in Settings. Sizes are for 5-bit quantised files; speeds are rough guides on a mid-range 2024 phone (8-core, 8 GB RAM) and will be measured in the prototype.

tiny.en≈ 31 MB≈ 6 minRough; fine for live previewOld or low-RAM phones

Alternatives considered: Vosk (small and streams well, weaker on accents; kept as an option for live preview), Android's on-device SpeechRecognizer (API 31+, built for live dictation, cannot take files reliably), cloud APIs (ruled out: need signal and send audio off the phone).

Architecture

  • UI: Jetpack Compose, Material 3, one activity, Navigation Compose.
  • State: MVVM with ViewModels and Kotlin Flow; Hilt for wiring.
  • Storage: Room (SQLite) with an FTS4 table for full-text search.
  • Background work: WorkManager running a foreground service (mediaProcessing type on Android 15+, dataSync below). One job at a time; others queue.
  • Recording: AudioRecord at 16 kHz, saved as Opus (≈ 7 MB per hour) with a foreground microphone service.
  • Playback: Media3 ExoPlayer, synced to the transcript.
  • Import: Storage Access Framework picker and the Android Photo Picker (for gallery videos), plus a share-target intent filter for audio/* and video/*.
  • Video: only the audio track is kept, re-encoded to Opus. A 1 GB phone video becomes about 7 MB per hour of sound. A thumbnail frame is saved for the library. The original video is opened again from its URI when the user wants to watch it with subtitles.
  • Native: whisper.cpp built with the NDK for arm64-v8a, using NEON and 4 threads.

Permissions and privacy

  • RECORD_AUDIO, asked only when the user first taps Record.
  • POST_NOTIFICATIONS (Android 13+) for the progress notification.
  • FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MICROPHONE and FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MEDIA_PROCESSING.
  • INTERNET is used only to download the model. No analytics, no accounts, no audio or text ever uploaded.
  • No storage permission needed: imported audio, and the sound pulled from videos, is copied into the app's private folder. Videos are read through the picker, never scanned.

Edge cases to handle

  • Phone call during recording: pause automatically on audio-focus loss, resume after.
  • App killed or phone restarted mid-job: WorkManager resumes from the last saved segment, not from zero.
  • Low battery: pause long jobs below 15% unless charging; offer "only while charging" in Settings.
  • Low storage: check free space before recording or downloading a model, and say how much is needed.
  • Video with no sound track: say "This video has no audio" at import, instead of queuing it.
  • Video with several audio tracks (e.g. dubbed films): use the first, and let the user pick another from the recording's menu.
  • Very large videos: extract audio as a stream, so a 4 GB file never has to be copied or held in memory.
  • Speech that isn't English: the English models will produce poor text. If most of a transcript is low-confidence, show "This may not be English speech" instead of silently saving nonsense.
  • Hot phone: drop to 2 threads when the system reports thermal throttling.

Build plan

Weeks 1–2

Prove the engine

whisper.cpp running from Kotlin on a real phone; transcribe a bundled WAV; measure speed and memory for tiny, base and small.

Weeks 3–5

MVP

Import an audio or video file, background transcription with progress, library list, transcript view with playback, export as text.

Weeks 6–7

Recording

In-app recorder, bookmarks, share-into-app, SRT and VTT export, search.

Weeks 8–9

Polish and release

Editing, low-confidence highlighting, dark theme, accessibility pass, internal test track on Google Play.

Later

Nice to have

Speaker labels (diarisation), live preview with Vosk, other languages (swap in the multilingual models and add a language picker), summary of long recordings.

Decisions

  • Standalone app, with its own code, package name and Play Store listing. Not part of the library app.
  • English only for the first release, using the .en Whisper models. The code keeps the model choice in one place so other languages can be added later.

Still open

  • Are speaker labels ("Speaker 1 / Speaker 2") needed in the first release? On-device diarisation adds size and effort. Until decided, the mockups show them but the build plan puts them under "Later".
